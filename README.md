**Geographic Scope:** Los Angeles County, California

## Project Overview

How do environmental conditions and socioeconomic disadvantages overlap across communities in Los Angeles County?

This project examined the **Indoor Air Quality (IAQ) Sensitivity Index** to explore how environmental, health, housing, and socioeconomic indicators intersect across Los Angeles County. Our team analyzed community-level patterns to identify areas with multiple indicators of potential vulnerability and develop data-informed recommendations for further investigation.

The analysis focused on census tracts ranking in the 90th percentile of IAQ sensitivity, representing 246 high-sensitivity communities in the team's analysis.

## Research Questions

- Which communities have high indoor air quality sensitivity?
- Do communities with greater educational disadvantage also tend to have higher IAQ sensitivity?
- Do communities with higher unemployment rates also tend to have higher IAQ sensitivity?
- Which communities rank highly across educational disadvantage, unemployment, and IAQ sensitivity?
- How can these findings inform environmental health equity and community interventions?

## My Contributions: Education & Unemployment Analysis

I focused on examining the relationship between socioeconomic indicators and indoor air quality sensitivity.

My contributions included:

- **Data analysis:** Used Excel pivot tables and visualizations to compare educational disadvantage, unemployment, and IAQ sensitivity.
- **Trend interpretation:** Examined scatter plots and trendlines to assess whether higher educational disadvantage or unemployment tended to coincide with higher IAQ sensitivity.
- **Community comparisons:** Compared percentile rankings to identify communities with overlapping socioeconomic and environmental vulnerability indicators.
- **Data storytelling:** Translated findings into presentation slides, speaker notes, and written analysis for a team presentation and report.

## Tools & Methodology

**Excel**
- Pivot tables and comparative analysis
- Data exploration and interpretation
- Scatter plots and trendline analysis
- Community-level percentile comparisons

**Analytical approach**
- Examined educational disadvantage, measured as the percentage of adults over age 25 without a high school diploma.
- Compared unemployment rates and relative unemployment percentiles with IAQ sensitivity measures.
- Evaluated patterns across communities rather than assuming the same relationship applied everywhere.
- Used percentile rankings to compare relative positions across Los Angeles County census tracts.

## Broader Team Findings

Beyond my education and unemployment analysis, the team examined environmental pollution, poverty, housing characteristics, and health-related indicators.

Examples from the broader project included:

- **Pollutants:** Nitrogen dioxide was elevated at the selected 90th-percentile cutoff in 99 of the 246 high-sensitivity communities.
- **Environmental and socioeconomic overlap:** 89 communities were identified as having both at least one elevated pollutant indicator and high poverty.
- **Housing:** Older housing stock was associated with higher children's lead-risk scores in the analysis.
- **Community vulnerability:** The team examined how environmental, socioeconomic, housing, and health indicators could overlap geographically.

These findings provided broader context for interpreting the socioeconomic patterns explored in my section.

## Recommendations

Based on the team's findings, we proposed several areas for further investigation and potential intervention:

- **Improve filtration:** Explore HEPA filter subsidies for older rental units and improved air filtration in schools near freight corridors.
- **Strengthen monitoring:** Consider fenceline sensors, environmental monitoring, and alerts about potential hazards.
- **Expand community participation:** Partner with residents, neighborhood councils, and local nonprofits to understand local indoor air quality concerns.
- **Support healthier housing:** Explore voluntary assessments for ventilation, moisture, and mold in higher-priority areas.
- **Use equity-informed funding:** Consider multiple vulnerability indicators when allocating environmental health and housing resources.
- **Track outcomes:** Repeat the analysis as new data becomes available to monitor changes over time.

These are proposed strategies, not interventions whose effectiveness was tested by this analysis.

## Limitations

- **Association does not establish causation:** The analysis identifies patterns but does not demonstrate that socioeconomic disadvantage causes higher IAQ sensitivity.
- **Community averages have limitations:** Census-tract averages can conceal differences between households and individuals.
- **IAQ sensitivity is not a direct exposure measurement:** The index represents relative sensitivity or vulnerability based on its component indicators, not direct measurements of indoor pollutant concentrations.
- **Relative rankings require context:** Percentiles describe relative positions within the analyzed data and should not be interpreted as the percentage of residents affected.
